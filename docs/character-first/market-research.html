Executive summary

Character First is an evidence-based sports character development program targeting youth athletes (U14 to Senior level) across Belgian football, basketball, and volleyball clubs. The program is delivered digitally, primarily through coaches, using a structured library of 59 exercises, 16 worksheets, and a dynamic plan builder.

This report benchmarks the program against the mature sports psychology ecosystem in Melbourne, Australia, then quantifies the Belgian addressable market, models revenue scenarios across three pricing tiers, and assesses scalability into the Netherlands, France, the UK, and beyond. The report concludes with five concrete strategic recommendations for market entry and growth.

Key finding: The Belgian youth sport market represents a TAM of approximately €14.2M annually across all three target sports, with a realistic Year 1 SOM of €85K–€140K growing to €450K–€800K by Year 3 under a direct-to-club and coach subscription model. The greatest near-term leverage lies in piloting with 10–15 football clubs through the KBVB grassroots network, then cross-selling into basketball and volleyball.

Section 01

Reference market: Melbourne, Australia

Melbourne offers one of the world's most developed sporting cultures outside the US. Its sport psychology ecosystem, spanning university clinics, federal programs, and private practitioners, provides a mature benchmark for what Character First is building in Belgium.

Why Melbourne?

Melbourne is the sports capital of Australia and consistently ranks among the top global cities for professional sport participation and spectatorship. It hosts the Australian Open, Formula 1 Grand Prix, AFL Grand Final, and is home to the highest concentration of professional sporting clubs in the southern hemisphere. Crucially for benchmarking purposes:

  • High coach-to-athlete ratio: Youth sporting culture is deeply embedded, with most suburbs having organised competition from age 5 upward.
  • Developed sport psychology ecosystem: Sport psychology is mainstream rather than niche. Clubs routinely budget for it.
  • University leadership: Deakin University, the University of Melbourne, Victoria University, and Monash have active sport psychology research programs with community-facing clinics.
  • Federation investment: Football Australia, Basketball Victoria, Volleyball Victoria, and Tennis Victoria all fund mental performance programs.
  • Digital adoption: COVID-19 accelerated the shift to online sport psychology delivery in Melbourne, normalising the model Character First uses.

Key insights that transfer to Belgium

Coach as primary lever

Melbourne's most scalable programs work through coaches, not direct athlete delivery. Character First's coach-licence model mirrors this.

Digital normalisation

Post-COVID, clubs in Melbourne accept digital-only programs as legitimate. Belgian clubs are on the same adoption curve, 2–3 years behind.

Price anchoring

Character First's pricing is 30-60% below equivalent Melbourne offerings, a strong value proposition in a more price-sensitive Belgian market.

Federation channel

Football Australia and Basketball Victoria distribute programs through club networks. KBVB and BBA offer the same leverage point in Belgium.

Evidence = credibility

Melbourne's program buyers (club directors, federation officers) demand evidence bases. Character First's scientific grounding is the right differentiator.

Section 05

SWOT analysis

A detailed assessment of Character First's internal strengths and weaknesses, and the external opportunities and threats in the Belgian and broader European youth sport market.

S Strengths

01
Evidence-Based Foundation Character First is grounded in peer-reviewed sport psychology and character development research. In a market where most competing programs rely on motivational content or anecdote, this scientific grounding provides institutional credibility, critical for selling to federation officers, club directors, and sceptical coaches who demand more than "soft skills" messaging.
02
Digital-First, Coach-Centric Delivery The platform empowers coaches to deliver the program. No external consultant is needed, no expensive on-site facilitator. This dramatically reduces friction and cost for clubs, and enables Character First to scale without proportionally increasing service delivery costs. The coach-centric model also embeds the program into existing practice rhythms.
03
Dutch/English Bilingual Content Serving Belgian Flanders (Dutch) and the international English-speaking market simultaneously without additional translation cost. This enables immediate expansion into the Netherlands, the single largest adjacent market, and positions the product for UK and Australian markets from day one.
04
Substantial Existing Content Library With 59 exercises, 16 worksheets, and a dynamic plan builder already built, the product has a ready-to-sell depth that takes competitors months to develop. The library's breadth across character themes (resilience, leadership, focus, team identity) covers the full season arc a youth coach needs.
05
Competitive Pricing Relative to Alternatives At €299–€999 per club/season, Character First is 40–80% cheaper than bringing in a sport psychologist and 30–60% below comparable Melbourne market pricing. This price-to-value ratio is a powerful sales argument, particularly for budget-constrained grassroots clubs who currently have zero professional support.
06
Multiple Entry Points / Tiered Pricing The product range (Coach Maand at €49, Coach Year at €299, The Week at €699, The Season at €999) allows Character First to acquire customers at different budget levels and upsell over time. The €49/month entry point removes the risk barrier for trial, critical for early adoption in a market with no product awareness.
07
First-Mover Advantage in a Whitespace Market No direct competitor is currently offering a comparable product in Belgium at any price point. First movers in B2B niche SaaS and sport programs typically capture 30-50% market share before meaningful competition enters, a 12-24 month window to build brand loyalty, federation relationships, and case studies.

W Weaknesses

01
Solo founder: bandwidth constraint Building product, selling to clubs, managing federation relationships, creating content, running social media, and delivering customer support simultaneously is unsustainable for one person. Without delegation or a co-founder, growth will hit a ceiling quickly, typically within 12-18 months of commercial launch, unless the model is designed to be self-service from the outset.
02
Zero Brand Recognition at Launch Belgian sports clubs have no prior awareness of Character First. Building trust in a coaching tool requires social proof: testimonials, case studies, visible early adopters, none of which exist yet. The sales cycle for a new concept in conservative sports organisations can be 3–6 months.
03
No French-Language Content Yet Approximately 40% of Belgium's population is French-speaking (Wallonia + Brussels). Without French materials, Character First is effectively locked out of this market segment and the entire French national market. Translation and cultural adaptation is a meaningful investment that needs prioritisation.
04
Limited Clinical/Academic Credibility Markers While the program is evidence-based, it has not yet been formally validated through a published Belgian or European study, nor does it carry endorsement from a recognisable sport psychology institution (e.g. KU Leuven Sport Psychologie Lab, European Federation of Sport Psychology). Such endorsements are gatekeepers for federation adoption.
05
No Existing Sales / Distribution Infrastructure The program currently relies entirely on inbound or direct outreach. There is no affiliate network, no federation endorsement channel, no referral program, and no reseller or partner ecosystem to drive volume. In a relationship-driven Belgian sports market, this is a significant go-to-market gap.
06
Technology Platform Dependency Risk If the platform is built on third-party tools (e.g. Notion, Airtable, Kajabi, or a no-code stack), there are risks around vendor lock-in, pricing changes, and feature limitations as the product scales. A proprietary SaaS platform will eventually be necessary for club dashboards, tracking, and federation integrations.
07
Limited Retention Mechanism / Outcome Measurement Without measurable athlete/team outcomes (pre/post surveys, coach-reported character improvements, team cohesion data), it is difficult to demonstrate ROI to club directors renewing licenses or federations considering endorsement. This limits testimonial quality and renewal rates.

O Opportunities

01
Post-COVID Mental Health Investment in Youth Sport The COVID-19 pandemic created a well-documented mental health crisis among youth athletes: increased anxiety, loss of identity, dropout rates surged across all sports. Belgian sports federations, Sports Vlaanderen, and club foundations have responded with specific budgets for mental well-being programs in 2024–2026. Character First can directly capture this funding wave.
02
Federation Partnership Leverage A single endorsement from KBVB as a "recommended coach development resource" could unlock access to ~12,000 youth coaches with one commercial agreement. Similar leverage exists with Basketball Belgium and Volleyball Vlaanderen. Federations are actively seeking low-cost ways to add value to their coaching licence portfolio.
03
Growing Public Awareness of Mental Health in Sport High-profile athlete disclosures (Naomi Osaka, Simone Biles, Kevin De Bruyne's post-injury psychological openness) have shifted public and organisational attitudes. Belgian clubs that previously dismissed mental skills as "not serious" are increasingly open. This cultural shift is in its early stages in Belgium. Character First can ride it.
04
Netherlands expansion: low barrier, large market The Netherlands is the natural first international market: Dutch-language content already exists, the market is 3× larger than Belgium, and the KNVB / NBB / NeVoBo (volleyball) are modern, digitally-oriented federations with coach education programmes actively looking for new resources. A Netherlands launch in Year 2 could double revenue without significant product investment.
05
Coach Certification / Micro-Credential Trend The European coaching education landscape is moving toward modular micro-credentials and CPD (Continuing Professional Development) points. If Character First's program can be mapped to UEFA Coach Education competencies or the EQF (European Qualifications Framework), coaches can earn CPD credits, a powerful incentive driving adoption.
06
University Research Partnership (KU Leuven / UGent) A formal research partnership with a Belgian university, validating the program with a controlled study on Belgian youth athlete cohorts, would simultaneously produce academic credibility, media attention, and a data pipeline for product improvement. KU Leuven's Faculty of Kinesiology and Rehabilitation Sciences is an obvious target.
07
Sports Technology Funding Ecosystem Belgium has an active sports-tech investment ecosystem (Leuven MindGate, imec, Flanders Sports Technology cluster). Sport Vlaanderen offers innovation grants for sport organisations. EU Erasmus+ Sport funding explicitly covers youth character development projects in sport settings, a potential non-dilutive €50-200K grant opportunity.

T Threats

01
Established Sport Psychology Firms Entering the Market If a larger Dutch, French, or UK sport psychology firm (e.g. The Resilience Project, PCA Europe, SIRCuit-style platforms) decides to enter Belgium with marketing budget and existing brand equity, Character First's window of first-mover advantage narrows rapidly. These firms have sales teams, federation relationships, and investor backing that a solo founder cannot match on equal terms.
02
Federation In-House Development Both KBVB and Basketball Belgium have internal coaching education departments. If they decide to build their own mental skills content (perhaps by hiring a sport psychology consultant for a one-time content development project), they become a direct competitor who already controls distribution. This is a moderate-probability threat within a 3-year horizon.
03
Price Sensitivity and Budget Cycles at Club Level Belgian amateur clubs are predominantly volunteer-run with tight operating budgets. A €999 season program competes with club insurance, equipment, and facility fees. In a year of economic pressure, discretionary development spending is typically the first cut. Character First's €49/month entry point mitigates this but does not eliminate it.
04
Coach Adoption Resistance ("Not My Job") Many grassroots youth coaches in Belgium see their role as strictly technical: tactics, skills, fitness. Character and psychological development is perceived as the domain of parents, schools, or psychologists. Overcoming this mental model requires sustained education and compelling case studies; it cannot be solved by product features alone.
05
Regulatory and Privacy Compliance (GDPR) Any platform collecting data on youth athletes (U18) in Belgium is subject to strict GDPR rules, including parental consent requirements, data minimisation, and the right to erasure. A compliance failure, even a minor one, in a youth-facing product can cause reputational damage disproportionate to the actual risk, particularly with risk-averse federation partners.
06
Founder Burnout and Solo Operator Fragility As a solo founder, the business is entirely dependent on one person's time and health. An extended illness, personal circumstances, or creative burnout could halt growth at a critical moment. There is no redundancy in the operation, unlike a team-based startup or established organisation.
07
Content Commoditisation by AI Tools As AI-generated sport psychology content improves, coaches may increasingly use tools like ChatGPT to generate their own character development exercises, worksheets, and session plans. This threat is real but partially mitigated by Character First's structured methodology, evidence-base, and professional curation. Commoditised AI content lacks the systematic, research-grounded framework that institutions demand.
Section 06

Strategic recommendations

Five concrete, sequenced actions to enter and grow the Belgian market, ordered by priority and feasibility within the constraints of a solo-founder operation.

1

Run a structured 10-club pilot programme (football-first)

Before scaling sales, secure 10 football clubs across two or three Belgian provinces for a structured 12-week pilot of The Season programme. Offer pilot clubs a 50% discount (€499) in exchange for structured feedback, pre/post athlete surveys, and a public testimonial. Football is chosen first due to sheer volume (2,000 clubs), the cultural familiarity with performance coaching, and the KBVB's structural reach. Pilot data, especially measurable character outcomes and coach satisfaction scores, becomes the primary sales tool for subsequent club and federation outreach. Document everything: record a 2-minute video testimonial from each pilot club's head coach. This is the most important activity in Year 1 and should begin within 90 days of launch.

2

Pursue a single federation endorsement deal within 12 months

Target Volleyball Vlaanderen as the most accessible first federation partner. They are smaller than KBVB, have a more progressive coach education culture, and are actively seeking resources for their club-level coaches. Approach them with a clear proposition: co-branded "Volleyball Vlaanderen Recommends" endorsement in exchange for a modest annual licence fee (€5,000–€10,000) and inclusion in their annual coach education bulletin. This single endorsement creates immediate access to ~1,400 volleyball coaches, provides institutional legitimacy, and serves as proof of concept for KBVB and Basketball Belgium conversations. Prepare a 4-page endorsement proposal with pilot data, pricing, and a draft co-branding mock-up before the meeting.

3

Launch a train-the-trainer certification track in year 1

The Train-the-Trainer model is the most capital-efficient way to scale beyond solo founder bandwidth. Design a one-day (or online equivalent) Character First Certified Facilitator programme, targeting sporting directors, head coaches at larger clubs, or sports educators. Charge €599–€799 for certification plus €99/year content access renewal. Certified trainers then deliver Character First within their own clubs, requiring only the digital content licence. This creates a distributed delivery network, builds a community of advocates, generates passive recurring revenue (annual renewals), and solves the founder-bandwidth bottleneck simultaneously. Target 20–30 certified trainers in Year 1, primarily drawn from the pilot club network.

4

Apply for Erasmus+ Sport funding for a formal validation study

The European Union's Erasmus+ Sport programme funds projects that develop evidence-based tools for youth sport character and well-being. A Character First application, potentially in partnership with a Belgian university (KU Leuven's kinesiology faculty is an ideal co-applicant) and sports clubs from two or three EU countries, could unlock €50,000–€200,000 in non-dilutive funding. This funding would cover: a formal randomised controlled study validating Character First's impact on youth athlete outcomes; translation of materials into French and German; and a white-paper disseminated to European sports federations. The academic credibility produced by this study is worth multiples of its cost in subsequent federation sales conversations. Application windows open annually. Prepare a draft application in Q1 of Year 1.

5

Launch the Netherlands market in year 2 with a partnership-led entry

Rather than replicating the cold-outreach model in the Netherlands, enter through a partnership channel. Identify 2–3 Dutch sports organisations already buying similar content (e.g. KNVB Academy, NBB coach education programme, or a Dutch youth sport NGO) and approach them with a white-label or co-distribution agreement. The Dutch market is 3× the Belgian market in football alone and already uses Belgian Flemish sports content. The cultural and linguistic distance is minimal. A Netherlands entry funded by the Year 1 Belgian cash flow, with a single partnership anchor already in place, dramatically de-risks the expansion. Budget €8,000–€15,000 for Dutch market entry (localisation review, partnership development, minimal paid media test) in Year 2 planning.

Month 1: Identify and contact 20 target football clubs for pilot (LinkedIn, club websites, federation directory). Draft endorsement proposal for Volleyball Vlaanderen. Research Erasmus+ Sport 2026 application requirements.

Month 2: Confirm 10 pilot clubs. Begin pilot delivery. Draft Train-the-Trainer curriculum outline. First meeting with Volleyball Vlaanderen federation officer.
