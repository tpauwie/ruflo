1
Michael Jordan: "Flu Game" (NBA Finals 1997, Game 5)
"I knew I had a chance to win, and I wasn't going to let my body stop me."
❌ Incorrect / Likely Fabricated
The Flu Game (Game 5, NBA Finals, June 11 1997, Chicago vs Utah) is a real and well-documented event. Jordan played 44 minutes despite being severely ill and scored 38 points. However, this specific sentence cannot be verified in any post-game interview transcript, ESPN archive, or published biography (including Roland Lazenby's Michael Jordan: The Life, 2014). The language is bland and generic, atypical of Jordan's actual post-game quotes from that era, which were more specific and anecdote-driven. The most well-known post-game remark from Jordan was about Scottie Pippen helping him off the court, not about his own mental resolve in these words.
Recommendation Remove or replace. Use instead a verified paraphrase from a documented source, or use Jordan's documented NBC interview remarks. If you want to reference the Flu Game, describe the event in your own words rather than assigning an unverified quote.
2
Marcus Aurelius: Meditations
"You have power over your mind, not outside events. Realize this, and you will find strength."
⚠️ Uncertain: paraphrase, not direct translation
The underlying Stoic idea is genuinely from Meditations, specifically ideas expressed in Book VI and Book IX. However, this is a modern paraphrase or composite, not a direct translation from the Greek. Different translators render the relevant passages very differently. Gregory Hays (Modern Library, 2002), the most respected modern translation, does not produce this sentence. The phrase "Realize this, and you will find strength" does not appear in the major scholarly translations. This version circulates widely online but its exact wording was popularised by internet aggregators, not classical scholars.
Recommendation Keep the attribution to Meditations but label it as "paraphrase" or use the Hays translation of Book VI.8: "You have power over your mind, not outside events." (stopping there is more defensible). Alternatively cite the specific book and section (e.g., Meditations VI.8) to show scholarly grounding.
